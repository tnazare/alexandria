## 3. Headline (H3 — accuracy)

| Metric | catalog | native | Δ |
|--------|---------|--------|---|
| **Model-choice accuracy** | **0.950 [0.883–1.0]** | 0.833 [0.733–0.917] | **+0.117** |
| Consult compliance | **0.950** (57/60) | 0.850 (51/60) | +0.100 |

- **Catalog wins again on the hardest subset, and by a wider margin than random-20.** Model-choice
  0.950 vs native 0.833; the +0.117 gap and the catalog CI reaching 1.0 confirm the random-20 result
  (catalog 0.867 vs native 0.683) is not a fluke of one subset.
- **The gap is compliance, as before.** Catalog consulted 57/60 vs native 51/60. On sparse, native's
  always-resident catalog already consults well (0.85 — its best subset), yet catalog's resident
  *compact* catalog still edges it out by engaging the model on 6 more cells. Where either consulted,
  it almost always chose the gold skill, so accuracy tracks compliance for both.

## 7. Interpretation

- **Two subsets, same verdict: resident compact catalog beats native on compliance-driven
  model-choice.** random-20: +0.18; sparse-semantics-20: +0.12. The effect is robust to subset
  difficulty. The mechanism is the resident-selection structure (no retrieval decision to skip),
  confirmed as the lever the earlier Alexandria runs were missing.
- **The cost shape is unchanged and honest:** catalog pays a token premium (14–31%) and grows linearly
  in N, unlike Alexandria. That is the tradeoff the hierarchical-disclosure prototype attacks (see
  `results/token-scaling-hierarchical-20261002.md`): keep the resident-selection win, bend resident
  cost back to ~flat by making only ~√N category headers resident.

## 8. Threats to validity

- N=20, nrep=3, one model (haiku/low). CIs quantify LLM variance; two subsets now corroborate, but a
  larger N and a stronger model would further harden the claim.
- Native consult/choice from transcript (direct); catalog chosen-id + consult captured server-side by
  the local fetch server (direct). Token cost is the modeled `cl100k_base` proxy for both.
- Forced-consult, decision-only regime held identical across conditions. Some sparse gold labels are
  vague, penalizing both conditions equally.
