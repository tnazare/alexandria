## 3. Token cost (H1)

| N (subset, mode) | Alexandria (median) | Native (median) | Ratio native/alx |
|------------------|---------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| 20 (sparse, hybrid) | 3712.0 | 3187.5 | ~0.86× |
| 20 (sparse, fts) [baseline] | 513.0 | 2915.0 | ~5.68× |

- Mean: alexandria 3341.5, native 3279.2 (means ~tied). IQR alexandria [200.0–5069.5],
  native [1739.0–4874.8].
- **Measured fact:** Under hybrid, Alexandria's median token cost jumps to 3712 — now slightly *above*
  native (3187.5), versus the ~5.7× advantage it held under FTS. The reversal is a direct consequence
  of hybrid retrieving (and so fetching) far more successfully: FTS frequently returned empty on this
  sparse library (median cost ~513, near the resolve floor), whereas hybrid finds and fetches a skill
  body in most consults. The token cost tracks retrieval success, not hybrid overhead.

## 5. Accuracy (H3)

| N | Cond | Model-choice (rate [CI]) | Recall@5 (rate [CI]) | Consult compliance |
|----|------|--------------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| 20 | alexandria (hybrid) | 0.517 [0.400–0.650] (31/60) | **0.895 [0.789–0.974]** (34/38) | 0.633 (38/60) |
| 20 | native | 0.867 [0.783–0.950] (52/60) | n/a | 0.900 (54/60) |
| 20 | alexandria (fts) [baseline] | 0.467 [0.333–0.600] (28/60) | 0.692 (27/39) | 0.650 (39/60) |

- **Conditional recall@5 = 0.895 (34/38): the biggest hybrid lift of the three flavors** — up from FTS
  0.692 on the same subset (+0.203). On a sparse library the gold skill is often phrased unlike the
  natural query, which is exactly where lexical FTS failed and the semantic leg helps most. It is not
  perfect (vs 1.000 on close/random) because sparse queries are genuinely the hardest to match.
- **Model-choice 0.517 vs native 0.867:** native still leads clearly (non-overlapping CIs). Hybrid
  nudged Alexandria's model-choice up from the FTS baseline (0.517 vs 0.467) as the recall gain
  converted a few more consults into correct selections, but the gap to native remains, driven by
  Alexandria's lower consult compliance (0.633 vs 0.900).
- **Measured fact:** Hybrid substantially improves retrieval on the hardest subset (conditional
  recall 0.692 → 0.895) and lifts model-choice modestly (0.467 → 0.517), but native's always-resident
  catalog still wins model-choice on sparse because its effective consult rate is far higher.

## 6. Interpretation

- **H1 (token): FTS advantage erased by higher recall.** Hybrid's median (3712) is ~tied-to-slightly-
  above native (3187.5), versus ~5.7× cheaper under FTS — because hybrid actually finds and fetches
  skills where FTS returned nothing. Recall/token trade-off, not hybrid overhead.
- **H2 (latency): supported direction, immaterial magnitude.**
- **H3 (accuracy): hybrid's strongest retrieval win, but native still leads as-selected.** The
  semantic leg delivers the largest conditional-recall gain here (+0.20), validating the design intent
  that semantic/hybrid helps most on sparse/unrelated libraries. Model-choice improved slightly but
  stays below native, limited by consult compliance (0.633) — a prompt-adherence effect, not retrieval.

## 8. Threats to validity

- nrep=3 reduces but does not eliminate consult-compliance noise; Alexandria's model-choice deficit vs
  native is now predominantly compliance (0.633 vs 0.900), as its conditional recall is strong (0.895).
- **Local embedding provider** (hashing/n-gram), not a production sentence model — the 0.895 recall is
  a floor; a stronger embedder via the HTTP provider could push the hardest sparse misses higher,
  with no code change.
- Native accuracy/token modeled, not scraped. Tokenizer proxy; single machine/model/client; N=20.
- Some sparse gold labels are weak/vague (e.g. t016), penalizing both conditions.
