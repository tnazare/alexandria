## 3. Token cost (H1)

- Mean: alexandria 1653.4, native 3173.9.
- IQR: alexandria [200.0–2516.5], native [1133.0–4849.0].
- **Measured fact:** On the sparse (unrelated-skills) subset Alexandria's median context cost is
  ~5.7× lower than native — the widest token gap among the three 20-skill flavors, and wider than the
  ~3.4× seen at nrep=1 for this flavor (native's median settled higher at 2915 over 3 reps). Sparse,
  unrelated skills force native to carry many distinct resident descriptions, while Alexandria fetches
  only the one matched skill (or pays just the resolve round-trip when FTS returns nothing).

## 5. Accuracy (H3) — STABILIZED

| N | Cond | Model-choice (rate [CI]) | Recall@5 (rate [CI]) | Consult compliance |
|----|------|--------------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| 20 | alexandria | **0.467 [0.333–0.600]** (28/60) | 0.692 [0.538–0.821] (27/39) | 0.650 (39/60) |
| 20 | native     | **0.817 [0.717–0.900]** (49/60) | n/a | 0.883 (53/60) |

- **Native leads clearly on model-choice: 0.817 vs 0.467**, with non-overlapping CIs — the strongest
  native advantage among the 20-flavors, and consistent with the nrep=1 reading (0.85 vs 0.55).
- The gap has two drivers. (a) **Consult compliance:** Alexandria consulted on 39/60 (0.650) vs
  native 53/60 (0.883). (b) **FTS recall when it does consult:** Alexandria's conditional recall@5 is
  **0.692 (27/39)** — materially lower than on close-semantics (0.957) or random (0.839). On a sparse
  library the gold skill is often phrased unlike the natural task query, so lexical FTS misses it even
  when invoked (the run log shows consult-but-empty cases, e.g. t016 returned `chosen=None` across all
  reps in both conditions — a genuinely hard/vague task).
- **Measured fact:** With nrep=3, native outperforms Alexandria on model-choice (0.817 vs 0.467) on a
  sparse library. Unlike random-20 (where the deficit was almost purely compliance), here Alexandria
  also pays a real FTS-recall cost — conditional recall@5 drops to 0.692.

## 6. Interpretation

- **H1 (token): supported, strongest of the 20-flavors.** ~5.7× cheaper median. Sparse (unrelated)
  skills inflate native's resident-catalog cost the most, while Alexandria fetches only the matched
  skill.
- **H2 (latency): supported direction, immaterial magnitude.**
- **H3 (accuracy): native wins on sparse, by the widest margin.** Two compounding effects — lower
  consult compliance (0.650) and genuinely lower FTS recall on unrelated/verbose queries (conditional
  recall@5 0.692). When skills are unrelated and resident descriptions are easy to disambiguate,
  native's always-resident catalog beats lexical FTS. This is the clearest "FTS recall weakness"
  signal in the dataset and the natural motivation for the semantic/hybrid modes.

## 7. Threats to validity

- nrep=3 reduces but does not eliminate consult-compliance noise; Alexandria's 0.650 consult rate is
  one of two drivers of its model-choice deficit (the other, FTS recall, is real on this flavor).
- Native accuracy/token modeled, not scraped (conservative for H1).
- Tokenizer proxy; single machine/model/client/subset.
- Some sparse-flavor gold labels are weak/vague (e.g. t016), which penalizes both conditions.
